Study note 03 · Digital Electronics

Half adders &
full adders.

Adders are combinational logic circuits used to perform binary addition. The main difference is whether the circuit accepts a carry-in bit from a previous stage.

Beginner levelCombinational logicRevision note

1. Half adder

A half adder adds two one-bit binary inputs, A and B. It produces two outputs: Sum (S) and Carry (C).

  • Sum: S = A ⊕ B
  • Carry: C = A · B

The XOR gate generates the sum bit, while the AND gate generates the carry bit.

Half-adder truth table

ABSumCarry
0000
0110
1010
1101

2. Full adder

A full adder adds three one-bit inputs: A, B and Carry-in (Cin). It produces Sum (S) and Carry-out (Cout).

  • Sum: S = A ⊕ B ⊕ Cin
  • Carry-out: Cout = AB + ACin + BCin

Full-adder truth table

ABCinSumCout
00000
00110
01010
01101
10010
10101
11001
11111

3. Half adder vs full adder

  • A half adder has two inputs; a full adder has three inputs, including carry-in.
  • Both produce Sum and Carry outputs.
  • Full adders can be connected in stages to build multi-bit binary adders.
Memory tip: a half adder starts a single addition without a previous carry. A full adder includes the carry coming from the previous bit position.

4. Quick practice

For a full adder with A = 1, B = 1 and Cin = 1, the binary result is 11. Therefore Sum = 1 and Cout = 1.
